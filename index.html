Working diffusers, not wall art. Every well depth comes from the quadratic residue sequence n² mod N (Schroeder), so a tile spreads a reflection into many directions and arrival times instead of sending it back as one echo. They do not absorb sound and do nothing for bass, room modes or loudness; for a trap tuned to one room mode, see the Bass traps tab. Deep tiles treat a room from about their design frequency up, once the wall is wide enough; block tiles are a shallow 2D diffuser for upper mids and treble. Formulas after T. J. Cox and P. D'Antonio, Acoustic Absorbers and Diffusers, 3rd ed., CRC Press, 2016. The band shown is design intent, calculated from the shape of the tiles, not a measurement. The ready-made tiles for the A1 mini, MK4 / Core One and P1S / X1 are also on Printables.

A trap for one room mode. A folded quarter-wave resonator, printed in blocks of 120 x 120 mm cells, each one tuned to a single mode of your room. It is a narrow absorber: it shortens the ringing of that one mode and does nothing for the other modes, the treble or flutter echo. For broadband bass, porous or membrane traps in the corners do more per kilogram of material; this is a narrow tool for one ringing mode. Every number on this page is calculated, not measured: nobody has printed these cells and measured them in a room yet. If you try one, we would like to hear your before and after results in GitHub Discussions.

Room

Your room decides which modes ring.

Walls

You rarely know this, and it matters: in the example room eight cells give about 1.5x faster decay with lively or medium walls, and about 1.05x with damped walls.
